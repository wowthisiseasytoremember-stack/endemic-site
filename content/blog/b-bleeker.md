## A Logistical Genius

Bleeker wasn't trekking through untouched jungles or funding grand, Indiana Jones-style collecting expeditions. Instead, his genius was purely logistical. He built an intricate network of local fishermen, market sellers, and colonial outposts across the archipelago to funnel specimens straight to his door. 

<BentoGrid cols={2}>
  <FeatureCard title="The Local Market Method" colSpan={1} image="/images/blog/b-bleeker-honor.webp">
    A massive chunk of his material came directly from local fish markets—the exact same stalls feeding the colony. Bleeker realized that the daily catch was basically a rolling, hyper-local survey of regional biodiversity.
  </FeatureCard>
  <FeatureCard title="Capturing Living Color" colSpan={1} image="/images/blog/b-bleeker-legacy.webp">
    When specimens arrived at his home in Batavia, he knew he was racing against the clock. If you toss a fish in alcohol, it fades to a depressing, uniform grey within hours. He commissioned local artists to paint the specimens while they were still fresh, capturing those iridescent, fleeting hues before they vanished.
  </FeatureCard>
</BentoGrid>

<Callout type="accent" icon="🎯" label="The Medical School Legacy">
  He wasn't just a fish nerd; he founded a progressive, two-year medical school to train indigenous medical assistants, teaching entirely in Malay.
</Callout>

## The Timeline of a Rebel

<Timeline>
  <TimelineStep index={1} title="The Rejected Application" duration="1819 - 1842">
    Born in Zaandam in 1819, Bleeker originally applied for a job at the Leiden Museum but was rejected because there was "no vacancy." To pay the bills, he joined the East Indian army in 1842.
  </TimelineStep>
  <TimelineStep index={2} title="Banished for Postage" duration="1847 - 1849">
    The colonial government actually banished him from Batavia. His crime? He publicly complained about the high cost of postage! They sent him to remote garrisons across Java.
  </TimelineStep>
  <TimelineStep index={3} title="The Silver Lining" duration="1849 - 1859">
    His banishment totally backfired. He used the opportunity to collect even more fish in completely new areas, vastly expanding his collection network across the archipelago.
  </TimelineStep>
  <TimelineStep index={4} title="Return and The Atlas" duration="1860 - 1878">
    When he returned to the Netherlands, he furiously published his massive *Atlas Ichthyologique*. By the time he died in 1878, his collection was bought by the very same Leiden Museum that had rejected him decades earlier.
  </TimelineStep>
</Timeline>

## The Famous Fish He Named

You might not know Bleeker, but you definitely know his fish. He named nearly half of the classic Southeast Asian species we keep in the hobby today.

<BentoGrid cols={4}>
  <SpeciesCard name="Clown Loach" latin="Chromobotia macracanthus" colSpan={2}>
    Described in 1852, this is perhaps his most iconic contribution to the aquarium hobby.
  </SpeciesCard>
  <SpeciesCard name="Bala Shark" latin="Balantiocheilos melanopterus" colSpan={2}>
    Described in 1850, captured from the rivers of Borneo and Sumatra.
  </SpeciesCard>
  <SpeciesCard name="Tinfoil Barb" latin="Barbonymus schwanenfeldii" colSpan={2}>
    A massive schooling fish that remains a staple for monster fish keepers.
  </SpeciesCard>
  <SpeciesCard name="Snakeskin Gourami" latin="Trichopodus pectoralis" colSpan={2}>
    A labyrinth fish that thrives in the slow-moving, oxygen-deprived waters of Southeast Asia.
  </SpeciesCard>
</BentoGrid>
