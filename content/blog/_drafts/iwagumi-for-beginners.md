## Outline

- **Search intent / hook:** Beginner wants to do their first "real" layout and is intimidated by iwagumi rules. Hook: "iwagumi is three stones and one ratio — everything else is practice." Position as the lowest-risk entry point into hardscape because it's minimalism, not maximalism.

- **Section 1 — What iwagumi actually is (and isn't):**
  - Definition: rock-only composition (iwa = stone, gumi = group), inspired by Japanese dry landscape gardens; no wood, no heavy planting mass — plants are the ground cover.
  - The odd-number rule: compositions built on 3, 5, or 7 stones; why odd numbers read as natural (no symmetry, forced hierarchy).
  - The size ratio: main stone : sub : accent ≈ 7 : 5 : 3 (state it as a starting ratio, not gospel — real landscapes deviate).
  - Focal stone placement: rule of thirds / golden ratio off-center; the tallest stone is never dead center; sub-stone echoes the main's angle.
  - Triangle principle: stones form an implied triangle; height differences create depth from the front view.

- **Section 2 — Choosing and placing your first three stones:**
  - Stone types for beginners: seiryu (classic, layered), ohko/ada stone, dragon stone (harder to place), lava (budget). One-paragraph pick: seiryu or ohko for first attempt.
  - Placement workflow: dry-place on a towel at tank scale before water goes in; photograph from front; adjust. This is the single biggest beginner win — you can move stones for free before glue/water.
  - Common first-attempt failures: all stones same height (flat), focal centered (static), stones in a straight line (no triangle). Show each failure + the fix.
  - When to use 5 stones: only after 3 works; more stones = more ways to look cluttered.

- **Section 3 — The carpet: Monte Carlo vs. dwarf hairgrass:**
  - Monte Carlo (*Micranthemum tweediei*): faster establishment, denser mat, tolerates lower light better, but needs steady CO2 to stay tight; grows upright if light/CO2 lag — trim discipline matters.
  - Dwarf hairgrass (*Eleocharis parvula*): finer texture, slower, more demanding of CO2 and light; the "pro" carpet that rewards high-tech setups.
  - Decision table: light level / CO2 / patience / texture preference → which carpet.
  - Planting technique: plantlets in rows with spacing, front-to-back so growth fills gaps; don't overplant (crowding = melt) or underplant (algae wins).
  - Substrate note: aquasoil gives carpets a head start (nutrients); link high-tech-aquascape-cost for the substrate decision.

- **Section 4 — CO2: the honest dependency:**
  - Carpets are the most CO2-hungry part of any scape — state this plainly; a Monte Carlo carpet without CO2 is a coin flip, with CO2 it's near-guaranteed.
  - Minimum viable setup: pressurized CO2 + diffuser, target ~30 ppm (test kit or drop checker); tie to the cost post for what that costs.
  - Low-tech fallback: if no CO2, swap carpet expectations — HC (Hemianthus callitrichoides) or low-light mats, and accept slower, looser results. Be honest about the tradeoff rather than selling a fantasy.
  - Photoperiod: start at 6h, ramp to 8h max; more light without CO2 = algae, not carpet. Cross-link the lighting/PAR post.

- **Conclusion / CTA:** Recap the recipe: 3 stones at 7:5:3, off-center focal, Monte Carlo for first attempt, CO2 if you want it to work reliably.
