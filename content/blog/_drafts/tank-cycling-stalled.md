## Outline

- **Search intent / hook:** A beginner mid-cycle sees flat ammonia and assumes failure. Hook: "ammonia not dropping on day 5 is normal — here's the exact test pattern that tells you when it actually IS stalled." Lead with the real post (16 gal, day 5.5, ammonia 4–8 ppm, 0 nitrites, 0 nitrates, Controsoil + Turbostart + RO) as the case study.

- **Section 1 — The nitrogen cycle in one diagram:**
  - Ammonia (NH3/NH4+) → nitrite (NO2-) → nitrate (NO3-), driven by two bacterial guilds (Nitrosomonas-type, Nitrospira-type).
  - Key fact to state plainly: the second stage (nitrite-eaters) always lags the first. That lag is why "ammonia drops but nitrite spikes" is the classic healthy pattern.
  - What "cycled" means operationally: dose ammonia → it hits 0 within ~24h AND nitrite hits 0 AND nitrate rises. That's the only finish line.

- **Section 2 — Why your ammonia plateaus (the actual question):**
  - Case study read: day 5.5, ammonia 4–8 ppm, 0/0/0. Interpretation: bacteria are establishing; ammonia consumption hasn't outpaced dosing yet. This is early-cycle normal, not stalled.
  - The plateau explained: if you dose to 2–4 ppm daily and readings stay 4–8 ppm, you're over-dosing relative to bacterial capacity — or under-dosing and reading residual. Dose correctly: target 2–4 ppm, re-dose only what was consumed.
  - Real stall signatures vs. normal slowness: no movement after 2+ weeks of correct dosing; ammonia never dips even overnight; no nitrite ever appears after week 3.
  - Temperature matters: cold water (<70°F / 21°C) slows everything; state the expected timeline range (2–6 weeks typical, longer cold).

- **Section 3 — What actually fixes a slow/stalled cycle:**
  - More time (most common answer — say so honestly).
  - Seed the filter: media from an established tank, or bottled bacteria (Turbostart-style) — set expectations: bottles buy days, not weeks; they don't skip the lag phase.
  - Surface area: more filter media / sponge than people think; a bare-bones sponge filter cycles slower than a loaded hang-on-back.
  - Substrate note: aquasoil (Controsoil) contributes some nitrification and buffers pH/GH — mention it helps but does NOT replace the bacterial colony.
  - RO water note: RO + remineralized water is fine for cycling; avoid chlorine/chloramine (RO solves this), keep temperature stable.
  - Don't do: adding fish "to feed the bacteria" (fish-in cycling is a stress story, not a speed story), mega-dosing ammonia hoping to force growth.

- **Section 4 — The checklist: when you're actually done:**
  - The 24-hour test protocol step by step.
  - First stocking rules once cycled: small hardy fish first, add in stages, re-test.
  - Bridge to next steps: link best-beginner-fish-planted-tank for what to stock, high-tech-aquascape-cost for the build this tank came from.

- **Conclusion / CTA:** One-line reassurance (day 5 flat ammonia = normal), the single most useful rule (dose to 2–4 ppm, judge by the 24h drop), CTA: download the cycling tracker sheet (log dates/ammonia/nitrite/nitrate); internal links as above.
