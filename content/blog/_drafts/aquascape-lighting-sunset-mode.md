## Outline

- **Search intent / hook:** Hobbyist wants the "cinematic" moody-tank look from social media and doesn't know if it's editing or lighting. Hook: "the sunset look is 80% physics (PAR + color temperature) and 20% staging — here's the real build behind one." Ground in the actual post: 10 gal, Seaoura 659 set slightly blue, frosted window film + Philips Hue behind the tank for the ambient glow, carpet of Monte Carlo + dwarf hairgrass with Anubias, Bucephalandra, and Ludwigia super red accents.

- **Section 1 — PAR: the only lighting number that matters:**
  - Define PAR (photosynthetically active radiation, µmol/m²/s) vs. the marketing noise (watts, lumens, "equivalent to X watts").
  - Practical bands: low (~50–100 PAR at substrate), medium (~100–200), high (200+) — map to plant categories: Anubias/Bucephalandra thrive low-medium; carpets and reds (Ludwigia super red) want medium-high.
  - Why the case study tank works: a 10 gal under a Seaoura 659 easily hits medium-high PAR at the surface; the reds and double-carpet demand exactly that. State the logic: match PAR to your hungriest plant, not your average.
  - Measuring: PAR meter vs. proxy (fixture specs + distance); be honest that most beginners estimate.

- **Section 2 — Photoperiod: the dial nobody turns:**
  - Start at 6h, extend by 30 min/week to a max of 8h; more hours ≠ more growth past the threshold, it equals more algae.
  - Consistency beats duration: same start/stop daily (timer), no random mid-day boosts.
  - The "ramp" trick: fixtures with dimming can fade in/out — mimics dawn/dusk, reduces algae shock, and sets up the sunset mode naturally.

- **Section 3 — Color temperature and the "cinematic" look:**
  - Kelvin basics: ~6500K neutral daylight (default for growth) vs. cooler/bluer shifts that make greens pop and reds separate.
  - Case study breakdown: Seaoura 659 set *slightly blue* — explain what a slight blue bias does visually (contrast between green carpet and red Ludwigia, depth perception in the water column).
  - The ambient layer (the actual "sunset mode" secret): frosted window film on the room window + Philips Hue strip behind the tank casting warm glow onto the backdrop. This is staging, not tank lighting — separate the two systems clearly so readers don't buy the wrong thing.
  - Recipe box: tank light (6500K base, slight blue bias, medium-high PAR) + room ambient (warm 2700K-ish backlight, dimmed) + dark surroundings = the cinematic frame.

- **Section 4 — Matching light to the plant list (worked example):**
  - Walk the case study scape plant-by-plant: Monte Carlo + dwarf hairgrass carpet (needs the PAR floor), Anubias + Bucephalandra (shade-tolerant, sit in mid-background where PAR drops), Ludwigia super red (the PAR + red-spectrum hungry accent that justifies the fixture choice).
  - Generalize: background/mid/foreground light tolerance map — put shade plants where they get less, hungry plants where they get more.
  - Failure modes: too much light + no CO2 = algae bloom (cross-link iwagumi post's CO2 section); too little = stretched carpet, pale reds.

- **Conclusion / CTA:** Recap the three dials: PAR (match to hungriest plant), photoperiod (6–8h, consistent), color temp (slight blue bias + warm ambient for the look).
