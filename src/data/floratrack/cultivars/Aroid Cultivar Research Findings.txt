Monstera

Thai Constellation Unknown (Thai lab) Disputed: 1990s / ~2020 Patented (stable) M. deliciosa mutation Highly disputed: Claims range from "found off a side of a road in Thailand in the 1970s" to "discovered in Thailand in the late 1990s" to "developed around 2020 through tissue culture". Most agree it was developed via tissue culture in a Thai laboratory and has stable variegation. Some sources incorrectly claim it's a hybrid with Epipremnum pinnatum.
Albo Borsigiana Unknown (mutation in cultivation) ~1920-1940 (speculated) Unpatented M. deliciosa / M. borsigiana mutation Disputed origins: Believed to have emerged as a spontaneous mutation in cultivation. One theory: discovered in a Berlin nursery (Borsigiana area). Another: a cutting brought to Filoli conservatory in California between 1920-1940. Most agree it's a chimeric mutation, not a stable cultivar.
Aurea Unknown Unknown Unpatented M. deliciosa mutation Spontaneous mutation in M. deliciosa populations, propagated by collectors. Origin not well documented.

Next Steps & Recommendations

1. Check IAS Cultivar Registry (aroidcultivars.org) for officially registered aroid cultivar names, originators, and dates.
2. Search US plant patents systematically for cultivars in your list—many patented cultivars have full origin and parentage documentation.
3. Contact specialty nurseries (Costa Farms, Gabriella Plants, etc.) directly—they often have internal records for cultivars they popularized.
4. Consult the International Code of Nomenclature for Cultivated Plants (ICNCP) for understanding cultivar naming rules.
5. Note: Many "cultivars" in the trade are actually wild-collected species given commercial names (e.g., Alocasia 'Dragon Scale' = A. baginda, Philodendron 'Silver Sword' = P. hastatum). These should be treated as species, not cultivars, for registration purposes.

Here's a look at the specialist nurseries you asked about, plus others that are key sources for rare aroid cultivars.

🏡 Gabriella Plants

A top-tier specialty nursery and a goldmine for originator data.

· Unique Cultivar: Developed the highly sought-after Philodendron 'Gabby', a sport of Philodendron Brasil. It's slow-growing and in very high demand.
· Origin Story: Named after the company and Gabriella Lane. It took over 10 years of selective cultivation to stabilize.

🏡 NSE Tropicals

Run by Enid Offolter, a legend in the aroid community.

· Authority: Boasts one of the largest personal aroid collections in the US. Offolter is a two-time president of the International Aroid Society.
· Uniqueness: Actively travels the world to find unique plants. A source for rare, well-documented specimens like Anthurium 'USBG'.

🏡 Glasshouse Works

A legendary, long-established rare plant nursery.

· Specialization: Known for aroids, begonias, ferns, gesneriads, and carnivorous plants.
· Provenance: Maintains excellent records on each plant's origin and history, making it a valuable source for cultivar data.

🏡 Kens Philodendrons

A massive collection with huge future potential for research.

· Scale: A conservatory in Florida with nearly 4,000 varieties. Plans to release 600+ aroids from a 30-year collection in 2026.
· Focus: Specializes in new introductions and propagation of Philodendron, Monstera, Alocasia, and Anthurium.

🌿 Other Notable Rare Aroid Nurseries

· FGMN Nursery: U.S.-based commercial nursery specializing in rare and collectible aroids.
· Brian's Botanicals: Kentucky nursery focusing on aroids like Colocasia and Philodendron, with a breeding program for hardy and ornamental varieties.
· Armstrong Aroids: Utah-based nursery focusing on a diverse selection of aroid plants for enthusiasts.
· Ecuagenera USA: Renowned vendor specializing in rare, exotic aroids, including sought-after Philodendrons.
· Peace Love and Happiness Club: 5,000 sq ft Seattle store with thousands of plants, including rare exotics.
· Aroid Greenhouses: Dedicated to providing high-quality rare aroids and Hoyas.
